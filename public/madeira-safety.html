Is Madeira Safe? Yes — There Are No Snakes

Madeira is the only warm European island with zero venomous animals. No snakes. No scorpions. No dangerous spiders. No predatory land mammals. This is not marketing — it is a fact.

Quick answer: Yes, Madeira is safe — one of the safest islands in Europe. No snakes, no scorpions, no venomous spiders. Sharks exist but attacks are virtually nonexistent. Perfect for families with kids.

What About Sharks?

Yes, sharks exist in the Atlantic. But incidents near Madeira are exceptionally rare — the last recorded attack near the island was decades ago. The reason: Madeira sits isolated, far from the major shark corridors. Local beaches, lava pools, and dive sites are considered safe.

Thousands of swimmers, snorkelers, and divers visit Madeira every year without any issue. If you're still cautious — the man-made pools in Lido and the lava pools in Porto Moniz offer protected swimming.

Crime & Everyday Safety

Madeira is one of the safest places in Western Europe for tourists. Violent crime is virtually nonexistent. Walking at night in Funchal, Lido, or any resort town is normal and safe.

Standard precautions apply: don't leave valuables in a parked car at trailheads, and watch your bag in crowded areas. That's it — no more than any European city.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are there snakes in Madeira?

No. Madeira has zero snakes — native or introduced. Not a single venomous or non-venomous snake species lives on the island. This makes it unique among warm European destinations.

Are there dangerous animals in Madeira?

No. There are no venomous snakes, scorpions, dangerous spiders, or predatory land animals. The only mammals you may see are rabbits, goats, and the small Madeiran lizard — all harmless.

Are there sharks in Madeira?

Sharks exist in the Atlantic but attacks on swimmers are exceptionally rare — the last recorded incident near Madeira was decades ago. Madeira beaches and lava pools are considered safe for swimming.

Is Madeira good for families with children?

Yes, it is one of the best family destinations in Europe. No dangerous animals, safe swimming, mild climate year-round, low crime, and a strong healthcare system.
